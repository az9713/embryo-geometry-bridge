A concrete energy norm for parameter-cell validation

A norm change alone does not certify useful material cells. This investigation does prove something narrower: the Hessian of the one archived weak-load, finite-quadrature model is positive throughout a displacement-coordinate cube of radius . Preserving the volumetric rank structure also makes a 0.1% gain predictor tube plausible. That tube is still a feasibility result: its complete residual, self-map and directed-rounding gates have not passed here.

The retained receipt separates the initial failed bounds, floating global feasibility screens, and an actual Arb certificate. The read-only script performs no equilibrium optimization and changes no physical model. The target is the original 611-free-coordinate, 12×6, strength-one field used by the root certificate, with and finite volumetric coefficient . No result here covers the seventeen revised-material fields or an unbounded material domain.

Common positive scaling of every force/energy coefficient leaves an unnormalized equilibrium equation unchanged up to a scalar. Fixing therefore removes that gauge and leaves dimensionless ratios; it does not remove the potentially large ratio , or bound the remaining ratios. An energy-norm calculation at numerical is not a result for an independently measured physiological compressibility and is not a uniform incompressible limit.

7. Actionable outcome

The validated positive-Hessian cube supplies a real local stability prerequisite. The rank-aware inequalities identify a concrete route to parameter-dependent tubes: validate the 0.1% gain case first, including its residual; then add coefficient directions with their exact affine derivatives, all failed boxes retained. A fixed-center nodal box and a parameter-dependent predictor tube are different sets and need separate proofs.

There is no justification for declaring that a finite set of local cells covers the full unbounded material domain. That requires either a defensible compact physical domain or proved asymptotic exclusions and a covering argument. No bounded material prior, continuum error bound, experiment-to-observer identification, cross-size validation, or novelty claim is supplied by this norm analysis.
