A pressure mean constraint is appropriate only for an actual constant-pressure nullspace; it must not be imposed blindly on a traction-boundary problem.

Taylor–Hood is a well established starting point, not a blanket proof of stability for every finite-strain state or weighted axisymmetric mesh. Verify its tangent inf-sup behavior on the actual mesh family and monitor pressure oscillation. A genuine stabilized or macroelement pressure method is an alternative if its stability and constitutive consistency are established. Merely adding P1 pressure to P1 displacement is insufficient. A three-field formulation is a defensible alternative, especially with an explicit isochoric/volumetric split, but changing that split changes the constitutive law and requires a separate retained model version.

The method is standard: the Ferrite nearly incompressible tutorial demonstrates mixed Taylor–Hood finite-strain mechanics; its volumetric energy differs from ours and must not be copied without adaptation. The deal.II step-44 tutorial documents a three-field approach to avoiding overly stiff displacement discretizations and traces it to Simo, Taylor and Pister (1985), DOI 10.1016/0045-7825(85)90033-7. These sources support the method choice, not a claim that locking has already been diagnosed in this project.

Acceptance before returning to biological transfer

  1. Pass zero-load, affine/isochoric and manufactured nonaffine benchmarks; verify consistent mixed tangents, pressure conventions, axis regularity and absence of a spurious pressure mode.
  2. At fixed finite , establish repeatable surface convergence and cross-formulation agreement. A small Newton residual verifies algebraic solution, not discretization accuracy.
  3. Separate a model choice (finite compressibility or exact incompressibility) from mesh effects. Report local volume distortion and its localization. A biologically admissible volume tolerance requires independent evidence; do not infer it from a desired answer. If exact incompressibility is selected, verify its mixed constraint directly.
  4. Recheck branch stability, outer-boundary dependence, contact/global injectivity and activation-interface refinement. P1 pointwise extrema cannot be reused unchanged for curved P2 elements; use certified polynomial/interval bounds if rigorous element-wide is claimed.
  5. Report empirical mesh uncertainty honestly. To call a continuum feature rigorously certified requires an applicable a posteriori bound or validated discretization and nonlinear-solution bound. Coarse/fine differences alone do not provide one, particularly for a plug-slice observer whose gap can be exactly zero.
  6. Only after numerical adequacy is passed, repeat small-only calibration under the retained revised protocol, freeze all material scenarios, and evaluate larger geometry. Keep static peak-proxy and unknown slice/time qualifications.

Adding baseline tension is physically motivated but does not solve these numerical gates. Its flat-reference first variation is balanced by the clamped rim, and contributes at small normal slope, whereas a stress-free area spring begins at quartic normal slope. This explains the different linear normal stiffness; it does not establish either biological adequacy or numerical accuracy. No novel mechanics principle or confirmed novel biological result is claimed here.
