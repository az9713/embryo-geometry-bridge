Originality audit: controlled epithelial furrowing

Date: 19 November 2026. This is an internal AI source audit, not external expert review or confirmation of novelty. Workbooks and specimen/data feasibility are audited separately; this document does not claim their calibration/validation gates passed.

Exact claim and decision

Candidate claim: one mechanically consistent model, calibrated only with18-micrometre activation responses and independently specified initial geometry/material information, predicts larger-region tissue deformation and bending without fitting the larger-condition outcomes.

Gate1 audit: PASS for this bounded comparison. GO as a retrospective quantitative model-transfer/reproduction challenge. NO-GO as a novelty claim if the target is only a scalar18-to50-micrometre shortening prediction. No new concept or successful spatial prediction is currently established. The same mechanism and qualitative size effects are already published. Absence of an exact match in this search is not evidence that the remaining quantitative-transfer question is original.

The distinction matters: predicting maximum cell shortening with a small-region regression is a different scientific task from predicting signed apical/basal displacements, curvature and regional bulging with a finite-height spatial mechanical model. Only the latter is a potentially substantive next validation target here, and its novelty remains unconfirmed.

Closest primary research and what it actually did

Countryman et al.2025: the actual source experiment

Endogenous OptoRhoGEFs reveal biophysical principles of epithelial tissue furrowing, Nature Communications,18August2025. Read the complete primary XML, particularly Sec5/6/7/8/10, Fig2/3/4/5 and Methods Sec15–17; final18-page Supplementary Information, especially Fig8/9 and Note pp13–14.

The paper already measures18-micrometre dose–shortening responses;37/50-micrometre furrowing/bending; activation-size/dose dependence; ellipse orientation; pressure-relief and basal-actomyosin perturbations. Fig4h explicitly compares18/50-micrometre shortening relationships and reports p=.73. That non-rejection is not a proof of equivalence. Repeating that scalar relationship is replication, not a new size-transfer concept. Sec7 Par29–31 and Sec8 Par33–37 already test intrinsic basal stiffness against extrinsic-compression explanations. The Supplementary Note concerns construct/activation strategy; it does not supply a calibrated spatial mechanical solver. No18-only-fit-to-large-region quantitative mechanical validation was located in the inspected article/supplement. This last statement is scoped to that material, not a universal absence claim.

Guerra Santillan, Dahmann and Fischer-Friedrich2024: directly cited mechanics

Elastic Contractile Stress in the Basement Membrane Generates Basal Tension in Epithelia, PRX Life2,013004. Read main pp9–10/Fig6 and Supplementary SectionI pp3–4, EqsS1–S7. This is Countryman's reference50.

The existing constant-volume cell model contains apical/lateral surface tensions and a basal elastic-area penalty. It derives weakly curved cell anisotropy from lateral-tension changes and shows this response vanishes when the basal elastic modulus vanishes. Consequently “lateral contraction plus a stiff basal layer generates bending” is established theory, not a new mechanism here. Its experiments concern a wing-disc basement membrane, whereas Countryman's early-embryo basal structure is actomyosin-rich: numerical stiffness values cannot be transferred between those materials without justification. This paper does not report the specified18-only Countryman calibration followed by larger-patch validation.

Worthmuller, Ziebert and Schwarz2025/2026: coupled signaling and mechanics across geometries

Modeling mechanochemical coupling in optogenetically activated cell layers. Online9October2025; final issue16June2026,125(12):3034–3047. Read final primary XML and43-page author article-plus-supporting-material PDF. The author's in-press PDF contains provisional March pagination; use final metadata for citation.

Eqs1–13 couple reaction–diffusion–advection, concentration dilution, Kelvin–Voigt plane-stress mechanics, an elastic foundation, bounded active stress and strain-sensitive signaling. Cell-doublet, chain and monolayer examples already address different geometries and propagation regimes, with parameter sweeps and different signaling assumptions. Thus a general claim of newly coupling optogenetic chemistry to mechanics, geometric transport or multicellular signaling fails. The model is principally an in-plane adherent-layer framework; it is not a validated finite-height out-of-plane Countryman furrowing/bending prediction or the specified locked small-patch calibration test.

Kumar et al.2024: inverse morphogenesis is not no-refit forward transfer

Reverse engineering morphogenesis through Bayesian optimization of physics-based models. Read final primary XML, main Table2/pp6–9, Fig3/4 and8-page supplement, particularly separate control/perturbed parameter tables.

Its Surface Evolver energy includes cellular springs/tensions, volume terms and ECM curvature energy; Bayesian optimization fits experimental target contours. In Sec9/Par36 and Fig4, the collagenase-perturbed contour is itself an optimization input, and new parameters are inferred to match it. That is inverse fitting of the perturbation, not the candidate's no-new-condition-fit forward test. The paper also exposes near-unidentifiable parameter directions from shape alone. Therefore parameter inference, shape optimization and nonuniqueness are established subjects; a good fit is not evidence that a small-patch calibration identifies the combinations controlling larger-patch bending.

Strongest defensible next claim

The nearest worthwhile target is narrower than a new biological mechanism:

After freezing a finite-height mechanical energy/force law, dose-to-activity map, boundary conditions and observation operator using only18-micrometre responses plus independently justified initial/material measurements, test a quantitative envelope of larger-region apical/basal profiles, signed bending and diameter/ellipse-orientation responses, without larger-condition parameter adjustment.

This is nontrivial only if the predictions are spatial/mechanical rather than the known scalar dose relation. Size crossover, basal stiffness and ellipse effects are already known outcomes. Their use to select the model class must be disclosed as retrospective knowledge; they cannot subsequently be advertised as prospectively discovered mechanisms.

Before a successful-transfer claim, show that every retained parameter combination consistent with small-region data gives sufficiently constrained larger-region predictions, or identify the additional measurement needed. A narrow-looking interval around one optimizer does not establish this. A small-response scalar curve can fit an activation amplitude while leaving basal elasticity, bending response, outer constraints or relaxation poorly determined. This is a required identifiability check, not an asserted impossibility theorem for every model.

Potentially stronger than a single successful curve would be a model-class-specific result showing that all small-data-compatible mechanical models either agree on a withheld spatial response or separate under an attainable extra measurement. Such a theorem would still need its own claim-level comparison with inverse mechanics and experimental-design literature. No novelty is asserted for it now.

Adversarial disqualifiers and decisions

  1. Scalar-only transfer: NO-GO for novelty. Reproduce Countryman Fig4h honestly; p=.73 does not prove exact diameter independence or causal sufficiency.
  2. Known mechanism rediscovered: NO-GO for claims that basal stiffness, volume conservation, lateral contraction or geometry-dependent bending are new concepts.
  3. Larger geometry supplied as the answer: NO-GO if a target curvature, final surface, prescribed displacement or condition-specific rest geometry is used to generate that same target and then counted as prediction.
  4. Hidden calibration: NO-GO for no-refit transfer if larger-patch outcomes determine stiffness, activation law, boundary constraints, onset thresholds or which parameter branch is reported.
  5. Conditional reconstruction relabeled forward: NO-GO if measured future areas/height are used in a geometric identity and the result is called an activation-only forecast.
  6. Fixed spatial mechanics with genuine small-only fitting: GO for a prespecified retrospective validation challenge. It may fail, be underidentified or agree with data. Performance is not known from this originality audit.
