Exact geometry certificates for the quadratic mixed solver

The verifier scripts/mixed_geometry_certificate.py uses exact rational arithmetic on supplied serialized node coordinates. Its claims concern the discrete P2 map, not the unknown continuum solution. It does not establish equilibrium, physical material adequacy, incompressibility, experimental prediction, or novelty.

4. Conservative boundary and global embedding certificate

Positive local determinants alone do not rule out distant self-overlap. certify_mesh therefore separately verifies:

  1. The reference vertex triangulation is a topological disk: consistent oriented shared edges, Euler characteristic one, one boundary cycle and path/cycle vertex links. Its P1 reference boundary is simple.
  2. Each interior P2 edge uses the same global midpoint index from both adjacent elements; distinct edges use distinct midpoint indices. Together with shared endpoint nodes, this makes the P2 trace continuous.
  3. The reference boundary is a flat axis rectangle. The current axis remains , and the rim has one fixed positive radius.
  4. Current top and bottom quadratic edges are strictly increasing in ; axis and rim edges are strictly increasing in , using reference-increasing orientation. For a quadratic edge with Bézier controls , the derivative is . Positive relevant coordinate differences certify strict monotonicity on the whole edge.
  5. Top and bottom span axis to rim. A sufficient separation condition is . This implies pointwise top-above-bottom on their common radial domain. It is deliberately stronger than necessary: overlapping global height hulls yield UNCERTIFIED even if the actual graphs are disjoint.

These conditions make the entire current boundary a Jordan curve, with top/bottom separated graphs joining the vertical axis and rim only at their endpoints. Together with strictly positive whole-element meridional determinants, they imply a global embedding of the meridional disk. Here is the degree argument, rather than an inference from a drawing:

Away from images of element edges, the map has isolated regular preimages and every preimage contributes to the Brouwer degree. The Jordan boundary has degree one inside and zero outside. Hence every regular interior image point has exactly one preimage and no exterior regular point has a preimage. Across a shared edge, continuity and positive orientation put the adjacent element interiors on opposite local sides of the shared regular trace. At an interior vertex, the oriented element sectors form a positive local fan: the local degree is a positive integer. A multiple fan or a second preimage would force degree at least two at nearby regular values, contradicting the global degree-one count. An interior point mapping onto the boundary would similarly create preimages just outside the Jordan domain. Therefore the piecewise map is locally open, globally one-to-one, and maps the compact reference disk homeomorphically onto the enclosed current disk.

Finally, preserves positive radial interior and the distinguished axis. Revolving an injective meridional map preserves the azimuth of each positive-radius material point and gives an injective three-dimensional axisymmetric body, after the usual identification of all angular coordinates on the axis. This does not exclude contact with an external vitelline membrane omitted from the model.

The certificate's reference argument is affine P1 geometry, with P2 displacement. It does not certify an arbitrary curved reference element. Its global boundary routine is specialized to a flat rectangular reference meridian; a curved or nonvertical-rim body must receive its own appropriate boundary check.
