Audit of a certified ambiguity in small-to-large mechanical transfer

The general idea is established: different mechanical parameters can fit the same data, and additional experiments can distinguish them. The sharper candidate is to demonstrate this under the exact finite-height model, complete declared small-region observation contract, and rigorous numerical error bounds. This audit has not constructed that witness pair, established a separating measurement, or confirmed novelty. It identifies the proof obligations and closest overlaps so subsequent computation can test a precise claim.

The audit is bounded: primary local papers and source data were inspected, targeted web searches were executed, and access limits were retained in the receipt. No numerical fitting, larger-outcome-based parameter selection, protocol editing, or external contact was performed.

The exact candidate claim

The candidate requires two witnesses , not merely two nearby least-squares minima. They must use the same discrepancy vector, allowed geometry/nuisance family, and observation definitions. A tolerance selected afterward to admit two desired witnesses would weaken the claim substantially. A common overall energy-scale change that leaves every deformation unchanged is also insufficient: it is a gauge ambiguity with no transfer consequence.

At minimum the existing 17-record table contains 68 scalar mechanical summaries: maximum shortening, region-averaged shortening, and separate apical and basal maxima. Matching maximum shortening alone is not matching all of them. The three illustrative apical/basal trajectories contain 63 timepoints, but lack a verified crosswalk into all 17 cohort identities. If the claim says all available small-region dynamics, those traces and a valid dynamic/time-reference model must also be addressed. A deliberately quasistatic claim should instead name the endpoint summaries it covers and avoid claiming full temporal compatibility. Neither a missing specimen crosswalk nor excluding dynamics from a stated quasistatic claim demonstrates that the data are intrinsically dependent or unusable.

The response must be a defined scalar or field functional with units and an observation operator—not an impression from two deformed plots. Examples include a signed profile contrast, a basal/apical displacement partition, or a source-compatible slice observable. A qualitative label whose allowed set is for both witnesses cannot supply separation.

This would prove that the stated small-data contract does not determine that larger response within the declared mechanical class. It would not prove that biology is fundamentally unpredictable, that the model is biologically correct, or that every small-data-compatible parameter has been found.

What would count as a rigorous witness?

1. Nonempty calibration compatibility. A common monotone peak contract already needs at least 3.131943715 µm uniform maximum-shortening residual for the canonical CSV; stricter concavity needs approximately 3.648762214 µm. These compatibility floors are lower bounds, not justified measurement-error allowances or evidence that any actual mechanical parameter fits all 68 endpoints. An empty proves model/contract incompatibility, not ambiguity between two members.

An interval straddling the tolerance boundary does not certify membership. Dose uncertainty, specimen-specific growth references, and allowed geometry variation must be given explicit quantifiers; they cannot be silently changed between witnesses.

2. Physical and mechanical admissibility. Require positive passive moduli, consistent prestress and initial force balance, admissible activation/control parameters, orientation preservation and noninterpenetration, the declared volume constraint, and a specified stable equilibrium/path. A small Newton residual or positive sampled Jacobians alone do not prove all of these. Multiple stable branches must either be carried or handled by a declared branch-selection rule. If the two witnesses use different initial shapes, say that the ambiguity includes geometry; do not attribute it solely to basal stiffness.

Observation-plane, initial-geometry or other allowed nuisance uncertainty must also be propagated into the response intervals before claiming robust separation.

Which extra observation is realistically worth testing?

The strongest first candidate is an uncropped, registered small-region membrane stack that measures the radial apical and basal displacement profiles or basal area expansion, alongside the preactivation AP/DV surface geometry. Countryman's membrane imaging and segmentation demonstrate an applicable modality. The public composite movie does not establish the necessary annotation accuracy or per-specimen profile itself. The movie audit documents the clipped apical edge and unresolved plane/calibration convention; those display limits must not be converted into new data.

The full shape field can constrain mechanisms hidden by four extrema; measuring initial geometry can independently remove a nuisance explanation. Neither statement guarantees separation for a particular pair. Before recommending an acquisition, evaluate the candidate extra observable on both retained mechanical witnesses, choose its sampling location/time by their forward predictions alone, and propagate all errors. If the remaining ambiguity is a force-scale gauge, more geometry cannot resolve it; independently calibrated force information is needed, as the epithelial inverse-mechanics literature already explains. Laser recoil without a drag/viscosity calibration is not automatically an absolute stiffness measurement. If the goal is robust discrimination between whole mechanism families and , optimize the minimum separation over their allowed parameters. Proceed computationally only after the common contract is explicit. If all candidate parameters fail it, report incompatibility.
