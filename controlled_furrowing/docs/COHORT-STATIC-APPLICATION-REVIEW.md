## Why the error transport is valid

For each free normalized coordinate, the admitted displacement is bounded by the exact affine predictor hull plus the **full declared corrector radius**. The exact difference between \(X+Pu\) and the rounded coordinates in the saved state is added. Fixed coordinates receive only their actual serialization gap. The physical length factor is applied once.

Let \(\varepsilon\) bound the physical coordinate error at the three nodes of every quadratic boundary segment. Its quadratic Lagrange basis has Lebesgue constant at most \(5/4\), and the sum of absolute derivatives on the normalized segment is at most 8. Hence the parametric coordinate error is at most \(e=(5/4)\varepsilon\), and radial-derivative error is at most \(8\varepsilon\).

If the base radial derivative is bounded below by \(m>8\varepsilon\), the perturbed radial coordinate remains strictly increasing. The actual endpoint radial degrees of freedom are fixed and have zero admitted radius. Both base and perturbed surfaces therefore cover the same radial domain; the declared ROI lies within it.

Let \(L_b\) be a bound for the base graph's slope \(|dZ/dR|\). At a fixed current radius, radial coordinate error changes the base height by at most \(L_b e\), while vertical coordinate error contributes at most \(e\). Thus

\[
E_b=\frac54\varepsilon(1+L_b)
\]

bounds the Eulerian vertical error. The verifier derives \(m\), \(L_b\), endpoint constraints and boundary-specific \(\varepsilon\) from the actual raw connectivity and node radii; it does not substitute a material-column height difference.

For top and bottom graph errors \(E_a,E_b\), the four admitted errors are

\[
(E_a+E_b,\;E_a+E_b,\;E_a,\;E_b)
\]

for maximum height shortening, regional mean height shortening, maximum apical downward displacement and maximum basal upward displacement, respectively. A supremum changes by at most the uniform function error. The fixed-current-radius disk mean has nonnegative normalized weights and obeys the same bound. Height uses both surfaces at the **same current radius**; it is not replaced by the sum of their separately attained maxima.

The current contract uses flat reference heights 33.5 and 0 micrometres, the same fixed current-radius ROI, disk weighting and spatial component maxima. Those settings were checked against the actual contract. They remain a declared static observation law, rather than an established match to the experimental temporal peak/ROI procedure.
