# Compose the centered L packets in exact signed coordinates

For the frozen original L cube, the two proposed compositions are valid. Use both and intersect **after preconditioning, row by row**. A third route keeps the actual affine predictor and uses its shared gain offset once. It needs the same completed packet set and no new integral evaluation. The remaining strict force-defect budget is about **7.02172036e-12** in normalized nodal coordinates. The previously reviewed all-168 defect bound is about **1.47980779e-9**, or **210.747×** that budget. ## The bulk identity

Use \(u\in\mathbb R^{611}\) in the existing free order. Full current nodes are \(Y=X+E u\), where \(E\) injects those 611 components into the mesh coordinates with zero increments on fixed coordinates. Write \(p(\theta)=u_0+S(\theta-\theta_0)\), \(Y_0=X+Eu_0\), and let \(D_B,D_S\) denote the assembled bulk and surface force defects \(F_I-F_Q\), projected onto the same free coordinates with the same normalization. For the bulk part, the only adjustable material coefficient in this slice is the common gain \(g\), through the frozen source dose times the element mask. Let \(K_B(u)=\partial_g D_B(u,g)\), which is independent of \(g\). On the existing box,

\[
D_B(u,g)=D_B(u_0,g_0)+\overline H_B(u)(u-u_0)+(g-g_0)K_B(u),\qquad
\overline H_B(u)=\int_0^1 D'_B(u_0+t(u-u_0),g_0)\,dt.
\]

This follows by varying \(u\) at \(g_0\) and varying \(g\) at the final \(u\). The box is convex, contains the original center and candidate cube, and has \(g_0\) in the unchanged gain interval. Continuity and positive material and cache strain domains justify the derivative over the closed segment. The interval Hessian box is convex, so its entrywise bounds also enclose the integrated matrix. Include all couplings among free entries of \(H_B\). Fixed coordinates can enter the point force and gain derivative through \(Y_0\) and \(Y\), while their displacement increments are exactly zero. The exact reference injection and material chart are already fixed. A coordinate representation change requires the already reviewed chain-rule, measure and rule identities. It makes no change to any coefficient or to the free projection. ## Use the three enclosures together

1. **Direct signed whole-box route.** Scatter all 144 reviewed bulk \([D_e]_{\rm centered}\cap[D_e]_{\rm prior}\) intervals and the 24 retained uniform surface intervals into one vector and multiply by exact signed \(R\). Every local intersection must correspond to the same exact force \(D_e(Y,\theta)\) on the same declared domain. The result encloses \(RD\) at every predictor, as well as throughout the original box.
2. **Global bulk point and increment route.** Scatter signed point bulk \(P_B\), bulk Hessian \(\mathcal H_B\), and signed bulk gain increment \([G_B]\). Keep \(R[P_B]+R[G_B]+R[D_S]\) signed. For the state term an easy valid row budget is \(\sum_{ij}|R_{ai}|\,|\mathcal H_{B,ij}|_{\rm up}\Delta_j\), where \(\Delta_j\) is any exact bound on \(|u_j-u_{0,j}|\). For a predictor-only residual check use the original 611 asymmetric predictor displacement intervals, without the corrector radius \(r\). For a whole-box force result use the original symmetric nodal radii. At the predictor the maximum absolute displacement is about \(2.002951995\times10^{-9}\), compared with \(2.063069217\times10^{-9}\) for the original box. A tighter state evaluation uses the signed product \(R[\mathcal H_B]\) followed by exact interval multiplication by each asymmetric displacement interval. The entrywise magnitude of that product cannot exceed the corresponding \(|R|\,|\mathcal H_B|_{\rm up}\) bound, since it retains signed midpoints until the final magnitude. Do not use the **combined bulk-plus-surface** Hessian bound to control a bulk-only increment. Surface Hessian cancellation can make the combined derivative smaller than the bulk derivative.
3. **Shared-parameter predictor route.** Use the exact binary \(S\) and the five common \(\delta\theta=\theta-\theta_0\) intervals directly. Assemble \([K_B]\) from each packet's signed affine-gain derivative. Form interval columns of \(R[\mathcal H_B]S\), then add \(R[K_B]\) to the common-gain column. Apply the same \(\delta\theta\) intervals to the resulting columns and add \(R[P_B]+R[D_S]\). This keeps the same signed gain factor in its state and explicit-gain terms. Any dependencies among \(H_B\), \(K_B\), \(u\) and \(\theta\) are safely relaxed because their enclosures hold over the full original box. The result applies only to \(p(\theta)\) for the root residual. It does not supply a smaller whole-box derivative bound.

For either signed matrix product, aggregate common signed coefficients before interval evaluation where inexpensive. When retaining the exact shared symmetric Hessian entry, the coefficient for \(\overline H_{B,ij}\) in row \(a\), parameter column \(k\) is \(R_{ai}S_{jk}+R_{aj}S_{ik}\) for \(i<j\), and \(R_{ai}S_{ik}\) on the diagonal. Summing the endpoint contributions for a unique upper triangle preserves cancellation that would be lost by treating \(H_{ij}\) and \(H_{ji}\) independently. Computing staged interval products is a safe cheaper first pass. For a few limiting rows, condition on each of the \(2^5\) corners of the frozen parameter box and use the exact predictor displacement \(S\,\delta\theta\) at that corner in the shared upper-triangle coefficient. Keep the signed gain term with its corner's fixed \(\delta g\). At each corner, minimize and maximize over the entrywise interval boxes. This gives the exact endpoint hull for this relaxed scalar row model because each fixed choice of Hessian and gain derivative entries is affine in \(\delta\theta\). It avoids unnecessary whole-matrix work if only a few rows prevent certification.

Take the exact intersection of all applicable signed row intervals, including the previously reviewed whole-box \(RD\) where the signed packets are available. Empty intersections require audit. Set \(d_+=\max_a\max(|\ell_a|,|u_a|)\) from the surviving row intervals. Avoid summing separately maximized point, state, gain and surface norms. If authoritative signed predictor intervals for \(RF_Q\) are already available, add them rowwise before taking the norm of \(RF_I(p(\theta),\theta)\). Otherwise the safe cached residual contribution is \([-\eta_0,\eta_0]\) for each row.

## Acceptance and center diagnostics

Keep the existing **combined all-168** Hessian defect bound \(b\) and \(z=Z_0+b<1\) for the original cube. With the unchanged cached predictor residual \(\eta_0\), the sufficient criterion is \(d_+<r(1-z)-\eta_0\). A direct certified bound for \(RF_I\) can instead be compared with \(r(1-z)\). Both comparisons are strict. At this checkpoint, reducing the derivative defect \(b\) all the way to zero would gain only about \(3.1141\times10^{-15}\) of force budget. The first target is therefore the predictor-force enclosure. Near-identity \(RH_I\) makes square \(R\) invertible, so fixed points are force roots. Contraction proves at most one such root in the cube. A failed self-map upper-bound test leaves existence unresolved.

A center diagnostic must enclose **actual** \(RF_I(u_0,\theta_0)\) by adding a hash-bound cached point \(RF_Q(u_0,\theta_0)\), the signed bulk point enclosure, and a valid surface point or uniform enclosure. A signed row excluding zero supports a nonzero displacement of the fixed-point map at that center. A midpoint of a wide force box supplies no sign or bias information. An independently certified lower bound greater than \(r\) shows that the center maps out of its old cube. For a nonexistence statement at that one parameter, a sufficient stronger condition is

\[
\|RF_I(p(\theta_0),\theta_0)\|_\infty>(1+z)r,
\]

because any old-cube fixed point would place its center displacement at most \(r+zr\) away. No such lower bound is assumed here.

A constant proposed shift \(s\) can be tested without fresh geometry if the **entire translated cube** about \(p(\theta)-s\) stays in the original one, for example by assigning \(r_\star>0\) with \(r_\star+\|s\|_\infty\le r\). With a signed uniform enclosure of \(RF_I(p(\theta),\theta)\), a sufficient translated residual bound is \(\|RF_I(p(\theta),\theta)-s\|_\infty+z\|s\|_\infty\), by integration of the derivative along the shift. The corresponding strict self-map test uses \(r_\star\). Shifting by a displayed midpoint is only a proposal. If the cube extends beyond the old domain, obtain fresh geometry, cached and integral derivative and residual bounds on the enlarged domain before using Banach.
