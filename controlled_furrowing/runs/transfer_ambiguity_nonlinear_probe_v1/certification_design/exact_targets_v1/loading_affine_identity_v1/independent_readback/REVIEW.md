# Independent readback of exact dose affinity

The fixed flat P2 force equation in the memo is correct. The code uses the squared second material column of the meridional deformation gradient, a reference-fixed element mask, and the stored reference measure. The exact target eliminates fixed displacement coordinates by its injection \(P\), while the full binary reference vector remains in \(D_ZX\). The fixed reference orientation and cylindrical weight keep the active coefficients independent of the current shape. For fixed references, gain and normalization, the dose derivatives are \(\partial_I F=g(Au+b)\) and \(\partial_I H=gA\).

On an unsplit affine material triangle the active stiffness and offset integrands contain two affine derivatives times one affine radius. Their polynomial degree is at most three. The simplex identity includes the Jacobian factor \(2|T|\); the degeneracy of the cylindrical radius on an axis edge is harmless for these polynomials. For positive gain the fixed-state Hessian is monotone in dose because \(A\succeq0\). This does not imply monotonicity of the passive Hessian along a moving state. The positive cache weights give a separately positive semidefinite Gram matrix. The cache coefficient entries need not all be positive, and the exact-minus-cache stiffness has no general semidefinite sign. The proof does not identify the sign of the actual defect. The mathematical drive uses the exact dose and gain, with rounded solver strengths serving only as numerical proposals.

At a smooth stable branch, write \(v=Au+b=\nabla V\) and \(H=\nabla^2_u E\) for the normalized potential \(E=E_p+gI V\). Differentiating the zero residual gives \(u'=-gH^{-1}v\) and \(V'=-g v^TH^{-1}v\le0\). The stationary normalized energy obeys \(E_\mathrm{eq}'=gV\) by stationarity and \(E_\mathrm{eq}''=-g^2v^TH^{-1}v\). For the unnormalized model energy multiply by the same fixed positive \(S_\mathrm{binary}\), or use unnormalized \(v\) and \(H\) together in the same displayed formula. No dose-dependent normalizer enters these differentiations. In particular, the actual active energy \(gIV\) has an additional derivative term \(gV\). These scalar identities give no sign for a surface displacement, maximum shortening, or another shape measurement.

The previously audited weak-load gain theorem covers the finite cached-quadrature branch at strength \(1+\delta\) with \(|\delta|\le10^{-3}\) for a single fixed original material and geometry. For the proposed negative-direction K–L loading pilot, the readback authenticated both separately observed exact-spatial local-minimum endpoint proofs, the same parameter singleton and positive scale, and all 21 matching binary operator members other than center and preconditioner. The nominal dose gap is exactly \(22159027/10^8\). The preconditioners may differ. None of these facts supplies an intervening bound or identifies one continuous branch by itself.

A whole stable path still requires every closed slab to pass the derivative, self-map, geometry and minimum tests with a common operator extending to each junction. At shared doses an enclosed root must enter a domain of uniqueness. Overlap of the geometric boxes alone does not suffice. The proof premises and identities are classical; this review establishes neither an actual loading interval nor a new general theorem.
