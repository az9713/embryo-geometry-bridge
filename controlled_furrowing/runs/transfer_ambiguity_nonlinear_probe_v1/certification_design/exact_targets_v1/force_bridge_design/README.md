# Exact force and Hessian bridge for the four fixed transfer targets

Design only. This memo identifies reusable kernels and requirements for a separately reviewed launch. No force, Hessian, quadrature sum, new geometry or solve was performed here.

## Operators and signed assembly

Write \(Y_0=X+\operatorname{inj}(u)\) with exact rational addition, and \(\mathcal B_r=\{Y_0+\operatorname{inj}(v):\|v\|_\infty\le r\}\). Each target has one singleton physical parameter tuple, zero matrix \(S\), and fixed strength \(d\,g\) at the exact optical dose and candidate gain. The solver's binary-strength field is the numerical seed. Evaluate both exact operators at the singleton strength using the exact stored binary material coefficients.

The binary-cache operator \(Q_{\mathrm{cache}}\) is the exact-real potential of the pinned frozen arrays, including its approximate reference maps, sites and weights. \(Q_*\) is an auxiliary rule using the **same normalized binary weights** with canonical P2 strain maps evaluated at exact reconstructed interior sites. \(I\) is the continuous **spatial integration of the finite-element energy** on the exact material elements and traces. Its energy normalization uses \(2\pi/E_{\rm binary}\). In each projected local derivative,
\[
I-Q_{\rm cache}=(I-Q_*)+(Q_*-Q_{\rm cache}).
\]
The first term applies signed exact moment defects \(\mathcal I-\mathcal Q_*\) to the analytic expansion and adds its two-positive-mass tail. The second term uses the same full \(Y\), including constrained coordinates, in both maps, with the same coefficient tuple at each site. A finite site difference or a canonical-rule moment defect alone is insufficient. Divide prescribed cache weights by the frozen positive energy scale once, and apply the resulting weight to the finite correction once. The continuous \(2\pi\) resides in the material moments; do not multiply the stored weights by it again.

Scatter all 144+24 signed local force vectors into the 611 positions and all local upper Hessian triangles into the symmetric global structure. Mirror each off-diagonal once; a free-DOF incidence is determined by the target projection, never the label of its source record. Compute \(R F_I(Y_0)=R F_{Q_{\rm cache}}(Y_0)+R D_F(Y_0)\) using the **signed** cached point rows and signed assembled defect. Compute \(b=\|R D_H(\mathcal B_r)\|_\infty\) as an upper bound, with \(R D_H\) formed by signed interval matrix contraction **before** absolute row sums. Preserve individually tagged point and whole-box operands. A point Hessian cannot supply \(b\), and the signed force at the point does not assert any uniform-in-material result. Conservative separate triangle bounds remain available as diagnostics.

### Material and source boundaries

Both the primary point bulk force helper and the whole-box engine use fixed \(\mu=1,\kappa=5000\). The singleton factory verifies these values for all four pilots. Other material laws require a separate review. The changed surface material coefficients, exact drive, active masks, normalized weights, trace widths and mesh coordinates are taken from each selected target. They may never be inherited from a previous B/L record. These four candidates have the same supported topology counts; there is no numerical geometry independence claim for other meshes. Those paths require original-cohort files and invalid radius/point premises here. Do not copy/rebind old globals or invoke old B/L sources as drivers. The old B/L reducers can contribute pure endpoint/scatter arithmetic only after a new admission validates the singleton semantics. The signed common-offset L/D reduction is unnecessary: all singleton offsets are zero.
